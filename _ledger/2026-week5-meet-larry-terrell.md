*By Ryan Mays — AAA Insider*

For more than five years, Larry Terrell has built one of the strongest résumés in league history. He has 50 career wins, a championship and a winning percentage above .600. Normally, *Meet the League* would explore how he became so successful. Unfortunately for Larry, AAA Insider also found his Facebook.

Statistics can tell you Larry won a championship. Statistics cannot explain why he once looked like the lead singer of a Christian rock band preparing to release its debut album exclusively through the church bookstore.

*Larry Terrell — **Soil of the Soul**. Featuring the hit single “Jesus, Take the Lineup.”*

There is absolutely no chance this man did not once ask a room full of teenagers to close their eyes while he played three chords and talked about his relationship with God. And somehow this same man would later become **King of the Crips**.

There is a lot to cover.

## From Dippin' SKOL to King of the Crips

Larry entered the league in 2021 as **Dippin' SKOL**, immediately announcing that he was a Vikings fan and therefore already comfortable investing emotionally in things that eventually disappoint him. He went 5–9. From there came **Giga Chad Barney**, followed by **The Prophet**, which is an outrageous name unless you immediately go 10–4 and win the championship. Unfortunately, Larry did exactly that, forcing everyone else to live with the fact that for one season the man who looked like the fourth-most-popular Christian recording artist at Lifeway was also the best fantasy manager in the league.

By 2025, Larry — husband, father of two young daughters and otherwise apparently normal adult — was operating under the name **King of the Crips**. Not King of the North. Not King of the Gridiron. The **Crips**. Larry looked at one of America's most recognizable street gangs and thought, *there's my fantasy brand.* To our knowledge, Larry's gang activity remains limited to waiver claims and occasionally driving through Wake County wearing Vikings merchandise.

The actual fantasy results are irritatingly legitimate. Larry won the league in 2023, went 9–5 the next year and followed that with an 11–3 season in 2025. AAA Insider spent considerable time reviewing the record in hopes of finding fraud. The fraud was disappointingly limited. Fortunately, Ryan Mays eventually arrived. In the 2024 playoffs Larry scored **160.14 points**, the kind of performance that should send a manager forward feeling extremely pleased with himself. Ryan scored **182.92** and sent him home anyway. Larry can be excellent at fantasy football. Ryan can simply be better.

The Vikings, meanwhile, have reached four Super Bowls and won zero. Larry reached one fantasy championship and won it, making Larry Terrell one of the more successful football operations associated with Minnesota. This is complimentary to Larry and devastating to an entire NFL franchise.

Larry also attended NC State and studied **soil and water**, which is phenomenal news for a publication tasked with making fun of Larry Terrell.

*Four years of higher education eventually led to this man calling himself King of the Crips.*

He now works in property valuation, so professionally Larry is trusted to determine what things are worth. Last Sunday he apparently valued ownership of his own fantasy-team name at roughly **four points less than necessary**. That is all AAA Insider needs from his résumé.

## A History of Being Carried

The most important thing we learned about Larry has nothing to do with fantasy football. Larry has apparently spent his entire adult life being carried around by other men.

## The Mangled Prison Pocket Incident

Week 4 is where Larry's story reaches its natural conclusion. He spent the buildup talking about punishing Ryan Hutchins, knowing full well that a 35-point victory gave the winner naming rights over the loser. Larry liked those terms considerably more before kickoff. By Sunday evening the swagger was gone and Larry was reduced to announcing, **“We will fight to not lose by 35.”**

They did not.

Hutchins won **142.28–102.48**, a margin of **39.80**. **Kenneth Skywalker** was dead. **Mangled Prison Pocket** was born. A man who had previously named himself **The Prophet** had somehow failed to foresee the arrival of Mangled Prison Pocket.

The humiliation did not end with the rename. Reflecting on the experience afterward, Ryan Hutchins told Ryan Mays: **“I found the matchup against Larry to be ‘pleasurable’. I hope you find your matchup to be the same.”** AAA Insider has no interest in unpacking the wording. We do appreciate the scouting report.

## Now He Gets Ryan

Ryan enters Week 5 at 2–2 after beating Zak while leaving roughly another starting lineup's worth of points on the bench. Larry enters immediately after losing both a football matchup and custody of his own identity. It is, once again, a complete coincidence that AAA Insider selected this exact week to examine Larry's life in detail.

Their history offers Larry little comfort. Even in 2021, during a season when Ryan was generously distributing victories throughout the league, Ryan still beat Larry **115.80–113.44**. Then came the 2024 playoff game: Larry **160.14**, Ryan **182.92**. Larry has beaten plenty of people in this league. Unfortunately, AAA Insider has confirmed that those victories cannot be redeemed for points against Ryan Mays.

Larry Terrell is genuinely one of the best managers this league has produced. He has a championship, 50 wins and years of evidence suggesting he usually knows what he is doing.

But *Meet the League* was never created to tell you that.

It was created to tell you that Larry studied dirt, roots for the Vikings, once looked like the fourth-most-popular Christian recording artist at Lifeway, briefly appointed himself **King of the Crips**, has spent an alarming amount of adulthood being physically carried by his friends, and currently manages a fantasy team called **Mangled Prison Pocket because another grown man made him**.

And now he gets Ryan Mays.

The timing of this profile remains completely coincidental.

The timing of Larry's next loss does not have to be.
