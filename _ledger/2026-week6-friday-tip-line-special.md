*By Ryan Mays — AAA Insider*

At 3:09 Wednesday morning, the AAA Insider Tip Line received a story about Zak Wesolek and a castle in Slovenia.

Five minutes later, a crab filed a complaint against the same man.

By that evening, God had weighed in on Louis Swanson, someone claiming to live with Chance Robinson had begun airing domestic grievances, and the newsroom was examining whether Larry Terrell might accidentally become the origin point of a worldwide fungal uprising.

**Twelve submissions. One day. Seven named subjects.**

AAA Insider launched the Tip Line to encourage investigative journalism. Instead, the league has apparently established an international witness-protection program for people who want to call their friends idiots under names like *Brosif Stalin*.

The newsroom has reviewed the October 14 submissions and organized them by the people who seem to have inspired this remarkable outpouring of concern. Some have been condensed. Material involving unsupported serious accusations, intimate details or threats has been withheld. None of the allegations below should be mistaken for verified findings merely because somebody had access to a web form at 4 a.m.

Welcome to **The Friday News Dump: Tip Line Special Edition**.

## THE ZAK WESELOK FILE
### A Slovenian cliff, a deceased crab and a childhood now under review

**Submissions: 3 · Sources: “A core memory,” “Rocky,” and “Cassidy”**

Zak Wesolek has spent the better part of this season being investigated for his fantasy-football decisions. Apparently, someone decided the research wasn't going back far enough.

A tip submitted as **“A core memory”** describes a childhood visit to a castle in Slovenia. According to the source, Zak's father made him hold his hand because the castle was built into a mountainside and there were dangerous cliffs nearby.

The source claims to have required no such supervision, an extraordinary achievement for a child whose primary competition was another child remaining safely attached to his father's arm.

There is no evidence Zak was actually about to fall off a cliff. There *is* evidence that, many years later, someone still considers successfully walking around a castle without holding a parent's hand to be a major personal accomplishment.

The second submission is considerably harder to corroborate.

A source calling itself **“Rocky”** claims to have been a Little Rock crab enjoying life near Bluff Point when a young Zachary allegedly picked it up and smashed it against a rock.

The crab narrates the story in the first person. It also reports being dead.

AAA Insider takes all witness statements seriously, but **conducting a follow-up interview with a deceased crustacean has presented logistical challenges**. At press time, the newsroom had not established whether the story happened, whether Rocky ever existed, or why this particular crab was apparently capable of typing.

A third submission, attributed to “Cassidy,” was too explicit and personally accusatory to publish as reporting. The newsroom has no basis to substantiate it.

What remains is a deeply unusual picture: Zak has been accused of needing parental supervision near cliffs and attracting hostile testimony from marine life.

**AAA Insider finding:** No charges filed. Rocky unavailable for comment.

## THE LOUIS SWANSON FILE
### His family has spoken. So has God.

**Submissions: 3 · Sources: “Peppa Jack,” “Laura Swanson,” and “God”**

Louis Swanson has had difficult fantasy seasons before. This may be the first time he has been criticized by an alleged relative, an aggrieved associate and the creator of the universe within the same hour.

A contributor writing as **“Laura Swanson”** claims to represent the family's point of view. The submission declares that most Swansons are respectable, hardworking people and that Louis is a conspicuous exception.

The source estimates the family contains **“17 or 23 people.”**

This is a troublingly wide range from someone offering authoritative testimony about the family. AAA Insider will refrain from drawing conclusions until the missing six Swansons are accounted for.

Another submission, from **“Peppa Jack,”** consists mainly of an increasingly aggressive assessment of Louis's attitude. The newsroom has declined to republish its threats. We would remind contributors that fantasy football is intended to provide entertainment, and that ESPN's waiver system generally offers fewer felony opportunities than the alternatives.

But Louis's most consequential critic submitted under a considerably more recognizable name.

**God.**

The writer identifying as God claims credit for humanity's inventions, including singing, machines, money and the internet, before arriving at a theological puzzle: if God invented humor, how else can anyone explain Louis Swanson?

According to the submission, **“La Porta Potty”** is sufficient evidence that divine comedy may have gone too far.

God also predicts further Sacko misery for Louis while inserting a suspiciously specific endorsement of expanding the league to twelve teams.

AAA Insider has not independently verified the source's divine credentials. The expansion proposal, however, suggests that even omnipotent beings understand the importance of lobbying.

For Louis, the problem is now bigger than his fantasy record. He has become the subject of **a family dispute, an attempted attitude adjustment and a possible celestial referendum**.

**AAA Insider finding:** Family count pending. God not available for cross-examination.

## THE LARRY TERRELL FILE
### “Lury da jinjur” and the impending mushroom apocalypse

**Submissions: 2 · Sources: “Brosif Stalin” and an anonymous contributor**

Larry Terrell was already having a difficult season. He has been renamed, profiled, mocked for his appearance and compared to an anime character. The Tip Line has now opened two entirely new fronts: the gym and his toes.

The first source, identifying as **“Brosif Stalin,”** filed a report under the subject line **“Lury da jinjur.”**

Its opening statement reads:

> “I go 2 da gym alot. I sumtim c jinjer n der too.”

The rest of the account alleges that Larry favors light weights, elaborate poses and a surprisingly modest relationship with actual lifting.

The spelling is difficult. The accusations are straightforward. The source believes Larry spends more time looking like someone who works out than working out.

AAA Insider cannot verify any of this. It can confirm that the source has developed an extremely adversarial relationship with the English language.

A separate contributor, requesting anonymity, submitted what is ostensibly a medical warning about Larry's toe fungus.

That description dramatically understates the document.

The source claims the alleged fungus is approaching a stage at which it could spread, develop something resembling collective intelligence and ultimately threaten human civilization. By the end, the report has stopped discussing feet and started describing a global mushroom takeover.

**No medical records, test results or other corroboration accompanied the submission.** This is not a diagnosis. It is, at most, an unusually aggressive pitch for a science-fiction franchise.

Still, the newsroom cannot help noticing Larry's predicament. One supposed witness says he lacks the strength to move a dumbbell. Another believes his toes could conquer Earth.

Both cannot reasonably be true.

Although, with Larry, the possibility remains open.

**AAA Insider finding:** Gym performance unverified. Global fungal takeover not imminent on the available evidence.

## THE CHANCE ROBINSON FILE
### The candidate's household stages a revolt

**Submission: 1 · Source: “Yam”**

Chance Robinson is undefeated at 5–0, campaigning for commissioner and assuring the league that he represents a higher standard of leadership.

Unfortunately, a new submission suggests his political problems may begin at home.

A contributor calling itself **“Yam”** filed a statement about a “human male” who answers to Chance. The source presents itself as an animal in Chance's household and describes a domestic operation in which Chance provides food, cleans up messes and occasionally endures the antics of a dog named **Bo Bo**.

Yam's outlook on its caretaker is not favorable.

The source expresses particular enthusiasm for the possibility of Bo Bo destroying furniture or creating another automotive disaster. It also openly anticipates the day Chance finally loses a fantasy-football game.

This is an extraordinary development. A man seeking the highest office in the league apparently cannot secure the endorsement of his own supposed household dependents.

We should emphasize that **AAA Insider has not authenticated Yam's identity or species**. But if the allegations are true, Chance faces a difficult question on the campaign trail: why should the league trust a man to manage ten fantasy teams when the creatures at his house are allegedly running an opposition campaign?

Commissioner Casey Hutchins, by contrast, has not been publicly denounced by a household animal in any submission reviewed for this edition.

Sometimes leadership is about the little things.

**AAA Insider finding:** Yam's voting eligibility under review. Chance's undefeated record remains intact.

## THE FRANKIE YENTZ FILE
### Six bottles of syrup and absolutely no explanation

**Submission: 1 · Source: “Not Chance”**

A contributor identifying as **“Not Chance”** submitted a story about Frankie Yentz involving grocery bags, suspicious discretion and what the source claims was a recurring purchase of approximately six bottles of Aunt Jemima syrup.

Six.

The source strongly implies the syrup was being used for purposes beyond ordinary breakfast preparation, but the newsroom has not verified those suggestions and is not republishing the intimate allegations.

What we are left with is a simpler and arguably more compelling mystery.

**What does a man need with six bottles of syrup?**

There are several reasonable explanations. A large family breakfast. A warehouse-store promotion. A genuine enthusiasm for pancakes. Perhaps Frankie simply believed the household was entering a period of unprecedented waffle demand.

The source, naturally, prefers a much less innocent theory.

AAA Insider will not pretend to know the truth. We will note that Frankie's famously limited GroupMe participation makes obtaining a detailed explanation seem unlikely.

He only recently discovered modern high-speed internet. Asking him to respond to syrup-related inquiries might be pushing the technology too far.

**AAA Insider finding:** Pancake explanation not ruled out. Breakfast investigation remains open.

## THE MACON MOORE FILE
### A roommate recommendation with catastrophic reviews

**Submission: 1 · Source: “The Gray Wolf of the Village”**

Macon Moore has recently faced scrutiny for a trade that helped strengthen Glen Hutchins' roster. Now a source claims his judgment was suspect long before he began negotiating tight-end deals.

A contributor calling itself **“The Gray Wolf of the Village”** describes living with a man identified as **“Boston,”** whom the source says Macon recommended as a roommate.

What follows is a chaotic account of apartment drama, unexpected visitors and a confrontation the writer compares to witnessing a *Jerry Springer* episode unfold in real time.

The most remarkable part is that **Macon does not appear to have been present for most of the incident**.

His alleged contribution was recommending the roommate in the first place.

This is a familiar pattern. Macon makes an introduction, someone else lives with the consequences, and several months later the rest of us are left asking how the decision was approved.

To be clear, the newsroom cannot verify the roommate story or the conduct attributed to people outside the league. Those details are not being presented as established facts.

But as a piece of league folklore, it contains the same essential ingredients as Macon's fantasy transactions: optimism at the beginning, confusion in the middle and somebody else holding the bag at the end.

**AAA Insider finding:** Roommate referral practices under informal review. Glen Hutchins has declined to return Brock Bowers.

## FROM THE EDITOR'S DESK
### Not every allegation deserves a headline

One additional October 14 submission concerned Gooey Johnson and made a serious, unsubstantiated accusation involving a minor. AAA Insider is not repeating the allegation or presenting its existence as evidence of wrongdoing. The source provided no corroboration that would justify publication.

Other submissions were abbreviated because they included graphic personal claims or language threatening real-world harm. Anonymity, pseudonyms and the ability to press “Submit” do not turn allegations into facts.

Our Tip Line remains open for stories, jokes, actual evidence, reckless political opinions and—apparently—posthumous testimony from local marine life.

The newsroom's position is simple: **you may submit anything; that does not mean we will publish everything.**

## THE FINAL TALLY

The October 14 haul produced **twelve tips about seven league members**. Three targeted Zak. Three targeted Louis. Two targeted Larry. Chance, Frankie, Macon and Gooey each received one.

Of the submissions we could responsibly mine for comedy, the resulting picture is deeply concerning.

Zak may owe an apology to a crab. Louis is facing opposition from both his alleged family and the Almighty. Larry has been accused of being too weak to lift weights and too biologically dangerous to remain near human civilization. Macon may need a background-check policy. Frankie needs a pantry inventory. And Chance, the self-appointed future of league government, cannot even be certain his alleged pets are on his side.

Somewhere in the middle of all this is an actual fantasy-football league.

AAA Insider intends to locate it eventually.

**Until then, keep sending tips. Preferably from witnesses who are still alive.**
